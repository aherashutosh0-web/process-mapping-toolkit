# Stakeholder Analysis Template (Power / Interest Grid)

*For figuring out who actually decides things, who quietly blocks things, and where your communication effort should go. Do this in week one and revisit it when the org chart changes — because the grid will.*

> **How to use this file:** list every stakeholder first (brainstorm wide — include the people affected but not consulted; they're the ones who surface at the worst moment). Then place each on the grid and pick a strategy per quadrant. The grid is only useful if you're honest about where people really sit, not where the org chart says they sit.

## 4. Strategy per quadrant

| Quadrant | Strategy | Effort budget | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Manage closely** (high power / high interest) | Involve in decisions, co-design, frequent 1:1s, give them visible wins. These people make or break the project. | ~50% of comms effort | |
| **Keep satisfied** (high power / low interest) | Brief them at milestones only; never surprise them; give them the one-page summary. A surprised exec becomes a skeptic. | ~20% | |
| **Keep informed** (low power / high interest) | Regular updates, demos, feedback channels. This group carries the change into the business and catches what testing misses — but don't let them set scope. | ~25% | |
| **Monitor** (low power / low interest) | Newsletter / all-hands mentions. Don't spend effort here, but re-check: people change quadrants. | ~5% | |

### Tips from experience

- **Map the doers, not just the deciders.** Warehouse floor staff have low formal power and can quietly route around any process you design. They're "keep informed" on the grid — and "consult in workshops" in practice.
- **Find the real influencer.** For each "manage closely" stakeholder, ask: whose opinion do they actually follow? Sometimes the decision-maker with high power takes cues from someone with none on paper.
- **A target stance of "champion" for everyone is a fantasy.** For some stakeholders, "neutral, doesn't block" is the win. Say so in the plan.
- **Stakeholders change quadrants** when restructures, budgets, or incidents hit. The change log (section 8) is how you notice before it costs you.
- **The "their win" row is the hardest and the most valuable.** If you can't fill it in, you have a persuasion problem before you have a delivery problem.
